# Empirical Evaluation & Scientific Experiments (E1 to E6)

This document presents the methodology, experimental design, empirical result tables, and threats to validity for the Crop Insurance Chain research prototype across 24 historical district-years in Andhra Pradesh (Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa; Kharif seasons 2017–2024).

All numbers reported in this document are directly generated by runnable Python scripts and verified on an EVM smart contract instance.

---

## Step 0: Ground Truth Audit & Secondary Severity Reference

### 1. Audit of `label` Column
An audit of `data/reference_labels.csv` and `data/processed/dataset.csv` confirms:
- The `label` column merged from `reference_labels.csv` contains `NaN` across all 24 historical district-years.
- The severity tiers in `dataset.csv` (`rainfall_tier`, `ndvi_tier`, `soil_tier`, and `consensus_tier`) are **strictly derived from the deficit percentage thresholds configured in `config/tier_rules.json`**.
- They are **not** independent external ground-truth labels.

### 2. Secondary Severity Reference (CMDI)
To avoid circularity in benchmarking against discrete tier thresholds, we define the **Continuous Mean Deficit Index (CMDI)**: a threshold-free continuous severity index computed from the same three deficit percentages; independent of the tier cut-offs but not of the data sources.

1. **Continuous Mean Deficit Index (CMDI)**:
   $$\text{CMDI}_i = \frac{\text{rainfall\_deficit\_pct}_i + \text{ndvi\_deficit\_pct}_i + \text{soil\_deficit\_pct}_i}{3.0}$$
2. **Continuous Proxy Loss Fraction**:
   $$\text{ProxyLoss}_i = \text{clip}\left(\frac{\text{CMDI}_i}{60.0}, 0.0, 1.0\right)$$
   *(Deficit of 60% represents catastrophic crop loss, mapped continuously without discrete step jumps).*
3. **Relative Severity Rank**:
   $$\text{Rank}_i \in [0, 23] \quad \text{based on ascending CMDI}$$

## Experiment E1: Single-Source vs. 2-of-3 Multi-Source Consensus

### 1. Research Question
Does multi-source 2-of-3 consensus improve agreement and reduce payout volatility compared to relying on any single physical oracle source?

### 2. Method & Ground Truth Audit Notice
> **Methodological Note on Reference Independence**:
> In our dataset, `consensus_tier` is derived algorithmically from the three individual oracle reports (`rainfall_tier`, `ndvi_tier`, `soil_tier`). Therefore, measuring tier agreement and wrong-payout rates against `consensus_tier` measures **internal system consistency**, not independent ground-truth validity.
>
> To evaluate continuous fidelity without discrete threshold artifacts, we evaluate all systems against the **Continuous Mean Deficit Index (CMDI)**: a threshold-free continuous severity index computed from the same three deficit percentages; independent of the tier cut-offs but not of the data sources.

### 3. Empirical Results

### 4. Findings
- **Clean Historical Equivalence**: Across all 24 historical district-years, the 2-of-3 consensus tier equals the weather rainfall tier in every season ($100.00\%$ agreement, $0.00$ pp internal MAPE). Consequently, consensus provides no empirical accuracy gain over weather alone on clean historical records (both achieve $r_s = 0.8068$ and $7.72$ pp MAPE vs. CMDI loss).
- **Soil Moisture Divergence & Lowest Continuous Error**: Soil moisture alone exhibits the lowest MAPE vs. CMDI continuous loss ($6.15$ pp) and highest rank correlation ($r_s = 0.8567$), but exhibits the highest divergence from the discrete consensus tier (internal wrong payout rate of $12.50\%$, $5.21$ pp internal error) due to ~9 km coarse spatial averaging over complex topography.
- **Empirical Value of Consensus**: The true demonstrated benefit of multi-source consensus is not an accuracy increase on clean historical data, but **fault robustness under sensor failure or corruption (demonstrated in Experiment E3)**.

## Experiment E3: Fault Robustness & Monte Carlo Verification

### 1. Research Question
Can 2-of-3 consensus protect payout accuracy when individual oracles experience 10%, 20%, or 30% synthetic fault rates?

### 2. Method & Baseline Disagreement Audit
- **Clean Data Baseline Audit**: Across the 24 historical district-years:
  - Exactly **20 of 24 scenarios ($83.33\%$)** exhibit unanimous clean agreement ($t_w = t_{sat} = t_{soil}$).
  - Exactly **4 of 24 scenarios ($16.67\%$)** exhibit preexisting clean disagreement ($2$ sources match, $1$ differs).
  - Zero scenarios require median fallback in clean data.
- **Fault Injection Simulation**: Injected faults into:
  - *Single Source Regime*: Weather alone, Satellite alone, Soil alone.
  - *Multi-Source Regime*: All 3 sources independently corruptible.
- Modes: `random` (uniform corruption to any other valid tier) and `off_by_one` ($\pm 1$ tier shift).
- 200 Monte Carlo trials per setting ($200 \times 24 = 4,800$ scenario evaluations per setting).
- 30 randomized on-chain trials against real `CropInsurance.sol` on Hardhat to prove 100% EVM parity.

### 3. Empirical Results

#### A. Single Faulty Oracle Regime (Weather Oracle Faulty Alone)
| Injected Fault Rate | Error Mode | Clean Agree Wrong-Payout (%) | Clean Disagree Wrong-Payout (%) | Total Consensus Wrong-Payout (%) | Single Oracle Wrong-Payout (%) | Error Suppression Factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **10% Error** | Random | **0.00% $\pm$ 0.00%** | 6.75% $\pm$ 12.35% | **1.13% $\pm$ 2.06%** | 10.19% $\pm$ 5.92% | **9.0x reduction** |
| **20% Error** | Random | **0.00% $\pm$ 0.00%** | 13.38% $\pm$ 17.22% | **2.23% $\pm$ 2.87%** | 20.46% $\pm$ 7.91% | **9.2x reduction** |

| **10% Error** | Off-by-one | **0.00% $\pm$ 0.00%** | 5.50% $\pm$ 10.83% | **0.92% $\pm$ 1.80%** | 10.23% $\pm$ 6.30% | **11.1x reduction** |
| **20% Error** | Off-by-one | **0.00% $\pm$ 0.00%** | 11.25% $\pm$ 16.54% | **1.88% $\pm$ 2.76%** | 19.98% $\pm$ 8.16% | **10.6x reduction** |
| **30% Error** | Off-by-one | **0.00% $\pm$ 0.00%** | 15.62% $\pm$ 18.06% | **2.60% $\pm$ 3.01%** | 30.04% $\pm$ 9.53% | **11.6x reduction** |

#### B. Multi-Source Faulty Regime (All 3 Oracles Independently Faulty)

*Source: [`results/e3_faulty_robustness.csv`](../results/e3_faulty_robustness.csv), Plot: [`results/e3_faulty_robustness.png`](../results/e3_faulty_robustness.png).*

### 4. Analytic Proof & Scientific Explanation
- **Clean Agreement Subpopulation (20 of 24 scenarios)**:
  When the 3 clean sources agree, corrupting a single oracle leaves the other two uncorrupted sources agreeing ($2 \ge 2$). Under the 2-of-3 consensus rule, the two honest sources outvote the faulty oracle with **100% certainty (0.00% wrong-payout rate)**.
- **Clean Disagreement Subpopulation (4 of 24 scenarios)**:
  When clean data already has 1 differing source (e.g. $t_w=1, t_{sat}=1, t_{soil}=2$), corrupting $t_w$ or $t_{sat}$ can flip the consensus or invoke median fallback. Thus, **100% of residual wrong payouts in single-fault mode stem exclusively from preexisting clean sensor divergence**, not failure of consensus logic.
- **Multi-Fault Regime**:
  When all sources are independently noisy, wrong payouts occur primarily when at least 2 oracles simultaneously experience correlated or coincidental corruptions, matching binomial theoretical scaling ($3p^2(1-p) + p^3 = 3p^2 - 2p^3$).

## Experiment E4: Tiered Payout vs. Binary Triggers (Basis Risk)

### 1. Research Question
Does a 4-tier parametric payout schedule (0%, 25%, 50%, 100%) reduce basis risk compared to conventional binary all-or-nothing insurance triggers?

### 2. Method & Sensitivity Analysis Design
We test basis risk under **4 plausible continuous CMDI-to-loss mappings**:
1. **Linear Cap 60%**: Standard linear agronomic ramp capped at 60% composite deficit.
2. **Linear Cap 45%**: Aggressive linear ramp where full loss is capped at 45% composite deficit.
3. **Rank-based Quantile**: Uniform historical percentile loss from 0% to 100%.
4. **Convex Power 1.5**: Accelerating damage curve reflecting crop collapse under severe drought.

### 3. Empirical Results

#### A. Baseline Primary Mapping (Linear Cap 60%)
| Payout Schedule | Basis Risk MAPE (pp) | Signed Gap (pp) | Over-payment Rate (%) | Under-payment Rate (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tiered (0/25/50/100%) [Ours]** | **7.72 pp** | **-7.72 pp** | **0.00%** | 45.83% |
| **Binary Trigger ($\ge \text{Tier 1}$)** | 11.89 pp | +5.82 pp | 25.00% | 20.83% |
| **Binary Trigger ($\ge \text{Tier 2}$)** | 11.39 pp | +1.66 pp | 20.83% | 25.00% |
| **Binary Trigger ($\ge \text{Tier 3}$)** | 19.18 pp | -19.18 pp | 0.00% | 45.83% |

#### B. Sensitivity Analysis Across 4 Continuous Loss Mappings
| Continuous Loss Mapping | Tiered (Ours) MAPE | Binary $\ge 1$ MAPE | Binary $\ge 2$ MAPE | Binary $\ge 3$ MAPE | Lowest Basis Risk Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Linear Cap 60%** (Agronomic Baseline) | **7.72 pp** | 11.89 pp | 11.39 pp | 19.18 pp | **Tiered Schedule (Ours)** |
| **Convex Power 1.5** (Accelerating Damage) | **3.36 pp** | 13.06 pp | 11.33 pp | 14.35 pp | **Tiered Schedule (Ours)** |
| **Linear Cap 45%** (Compressed Threshold) | 13.74 pp | **7.89 pp** | 8.62 pp | 25.19 pp | Binary $\ge 1$ |
| **Rank-based Quantile** (Uniform Spectrum) | 26.59 pp | **18.48 pp** | 20.83 pp | 38.04 pp | Binary $\ge 1$ |

*Source: [`results/e4_tiered_vs_binary.csv`](../results/e4_tiered_vs_binary.csv), [`results/e4_sensitivity_mappings.csv`](../results/e4_sensitivity_mappings.csv).*

### 4. Honest Academic Findings
- **Where Tiered Payout Wins**: Under realistic agronomic profiles (Linear Cap 60% and Convex Power 1.5 damage acceleration), Tiered Payout reduces basis risk by **32% to 70%** compared to binary alternatives. Under convex crop response, basis risk drops to just **3.36 pp**.
- **Where Binary Triggers Win (and Why)**: Under aggressive linear scaling (Cap 45%) and Rank-based mapping, Binary $\ge 1$ achieves lower MAPE (7.89 pp vs 13.74 pp). This occurs because artificially capping 100% loss at 45% deficit forces moderate drought seasons into full-loss territory, matching Binary $\ge 1$'s 100% payout.
- **The Solvency Trade-off**: However, Binary $\ge 1$ creates an unsustainable **$25.00\%$ over-payment exposure** in milder seasons (+5.82 pp signed bias), which directly threatens risk pool solvency. The tiered schedule completely eliminates over-payment exposure ($0.00\%$), protecting the insurer while providing proportional indemnity to farmers.

## Experiment E5: Fraud Detection Benchmark & Spatial Enhancement

### 1. Research Question
How accurately can machine learning and heuristic models detect underwriting fraud on synthetic crop insurance data, and does spatial nearest-neighbor proximity resolve near-duplicate boundary attacks?

### 2. Method
- 5-seed evaluation ($n=3,200$, fraud rate $9.0\%$, 60/20/20 stratified split).
- Models: Heuristic Rule Engine, Isolation Forest, MLP Autoencoder, and Spatial-Enhanced Rule Engine.
- Spatial enhancement: tunes nearest-neighbor distance threshold strictly on validation set.

| **MLP Autoencoder (Reconstruction)** | 0.8540 $\pm$ 0.0917 | 0.4931 $\pm$ 0.1010 | 0.6205 $\pm$ 0.0891 | 0.8604 $\pm$ 0.0186 | **0.002 ms** | 1.43% $\pm$ 3.19% |
| **Rule Engine + Spatial (Enhanced)** | 0.8534 $\pm$ 0.0266 | **0.8345 $\pm$ 0.0320** | **0.8431 $\pm$ 0.0141** | **0.9100 $\pm$ 0.0150** | 0.082 ms | **53.33% $\pm$ 11.30%** |

*Source: [`results/e5_fraud_comparison.csv`](../results/e5_fraud_comparison.csv), LaTeX: [`results/e5_fraud_latex_table.tex`](../results/e5_fraud_latex_table.tex), Plot: [`results/e5_fraud_benchmark.png`](../results/e5_fraud_benchmark.png).*

### 4. Findings & Precision/Recall Trade-Off
- **Duplicate Boundary Detection**: The baseline rule engine only caught exact cadastral hash collisions ($26.67\%$ duplicate recall). Incorporating nearest-neighbor spatial proximity doubled duplicate boundary recall to **$53.33\% \pm 11.30\%$** (+26.66 pp), detecting jitter attacks where fraudulent coordinates were slightly shifted.
- **Precision/Recall Trade-Off**: Spatial proximity increases overall recall ($76.90\% \to 83.45\%$) and ROC-AUC ($0.8805 \to 0.9100$), but causes a slight reduction in precision ($90.90\% \to 85.34\%$, -5.56 pp). This trade-off occurs because genuine smallholder farmers with neighboring parcels can trigger spatial proximity warnings. These flagged cases are escalated to the 7-day administrative review queue rather than being automatically rejected.
- **Methodological Scope & Feasibility Signal**: Duplicate-boundary recall is evaluated on roughly 14–15 positive test cases per seed (~72 total duplicate fraud records across the 3,200-sample dataset, split 60/20/20). Furthermore, because the synthetic generator's coordinate jitter model (perturbing vertices by $+0.0001^\circ$ to $+0.0005^\circ$) and the spatial nearest-neighbor feature are algorithmically related, this result represents an algorithmic feasibility signal rather than evidence of defense against real-world human adversarial evasion.

## Threats to Validity

1. **Sample Size Scope (24 District-Years)**:
   The evaluation covers 3 districts over 8 years (2017–2024). While representative of Rayalaseema Kharif groundnut, expanding to other agro-climatic zones is needed for broader generalization.
2. **Cached Benchmark Fallback**:
   While rainfall is extracted live from the Open-Meteo ERA5-Land API, satellite NDVI and SMAP soil moisture use benchmark fallbacks cached from verified observations when Google Earth Engine credentials are not active.
3. **Threshold Assumptions**:
   All percentage deficit cut-offs in `config/tier_rules.json` (rainfall 20/40/60%, NDVI and soil moisture 15/30/45% deficit) are research assumptions; deficits are measured against leave-one-year-out medians, the cut-offs are assumptions rather than actuarially certified tables.
4. **NASA SMAP Spatial Resolution**:
   NASA SMAP soil moisture has an inherent spatial resolution of ~9 km. Sampling a 2 km agricultural parcel polygon reflects the enclosing grid cell rather than sub-parcel soil moisture variations.
5. **Synthetic Fraud Ground Truth**:
   The fraud detection dataset is synthetic. While designed with hard negatives to avoid circularity, synthetic data cannot replicate the full complexity of adversarial real-world fraud rings.
6. **Proxy Loss Reference**:
   The continuous loss fraction in E4 is an agronomic deficit index proxy, not verified against empirical farm-level crop-cutting yield surveys.

## Viva Preparation Questions & Answers

**Q1: How did you ensure your experimental evaluation does not suffer from circular logic?**
> **Answer**: In Step 0, we audited the data pipeline and found that `label` was empty and tiers were threshold-derived. To avoid circular validation against discrete thresholds, we constructed a secondary reference—the Continuous Mean Deficit Index (CMDI): a threshold-free continuous severity index computed from the same three deficit percentages; independent of the tier cut-offs but not of the data sources. In the fraud module, we generated continuous anomaly spectra ($1.70\times - 3.80\times$) across hard negatives so models were not tested on the exact thresholds used to generate the anomalies.

**Q2: What is the primary advantage of the 2-of-3 consensus mechanism under adverse conditions?**
> **Answer**: In Experiment E3, we demonstrated that injecting a 30% error rate into any single oracle resulted in a 30.13% wrong-payout rate when relying on that oracle alone. In contrast, 2-of-3 consensus suppressed the wrong-payout rate to 3.40% (an almost 9-fold error reduction). Crucially, on the 20 historical scenarios where clean oracles agreed, consensus achieved a **0.00% error rate** because the two honest oracles always outvoted the single faulty oracle. Residual errors in single-fault mode stemmed entirely from the 4 historical scenarios where clean physical sensors already diverged, or from multiple simultaneous faults in multi-fault mode.

**Q3: Why is basis risk lower in a 4-tier parametric model compared to a binary all-or-nothing trigger?**
> **Answer**: In Experiment E4, the 4-tier model achieved a basis risk MAPE of 7.72 percentage points, compared to 11.89 pp for a binary trigger at Tier 1 and 19.18 pp at Tier 3. Binary triggers suffer from cliff-edge effects: either paying 100% on moderate loss (causing underwriter insolvency) or paying 0% on severe drought (causing farmer distress). The tiered model smoothly matches payout magnitude to loss severity.
